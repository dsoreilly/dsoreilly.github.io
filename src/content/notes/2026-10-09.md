## 1

The news that [Deno is joining Cloudflare](https://deno.com/blog/cloudflare/) just broke — the crucial part of this is that the Deno runtime will **no longer be actively developed**:

> We will support the Deno runtime for another year with monthly releases containing bug fixes and security updates. After that year we will end our development of the Deno runtime. Deno will remain open source, and we welcome others who want to continue its development.

I've always been a huge fan of Deno, in part due to the mascot and [community artwork](https://deno.com/artwork/), but mostly just because how ridiculously user-friendly it is. When I first used Deno, it just felt like a massive breath of fresh air — being able to just run **any** TypeScript file by calling `deno run` felt revolutionary. From my own experience, Deno nailed "it just works".

It was also one of the first (maybe _the first_) "oxidation" projects in the JavaScript ecosystem — fast-forward and it seems like _all_ JavaScript tooling is being written (or re-written) in Rust.

Becoming part of Cloudflare does feel like the natural progression for the Deno team due to their recent focus on server infrastructure, and their work on [celld](https://celld.dev/).

> We’re joining Cloudflare to make the Workers programming model a mainstream way to build servers. By bringing celld and workerd together, we want it to be radically easy to build and operate distributed applications on your own infrastructure.

<cite>[Deno is joining Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/deno-joins-cloudflare/)</cite>

I'll be a little sad to say "goodbye" to Deno.
